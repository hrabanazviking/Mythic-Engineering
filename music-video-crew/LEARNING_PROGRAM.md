# The Learning Program — How the Crew Gets Better

*Founded 2026-11-09, at Volmarr's word: study, learn, and tinker — have fun being intensely creative, practicing and perfecting the skills.*

In plain words: skill is not a thing we wait for; it is a thing we practise on purpose. This program is the crew's curriculum — what we study, what we drill, and how every lesson gets written down so the next video starts smarter than the last. Fun is part of the method: a crew that plays with the craft learns faster than one that only fears mistakes.

## Study topics

### A. Cinematography & the generated camera
- Shot grammar: what each framing *says*; when a wide carries a singer better than a close ever could.
- Camera moves the forge can actually do honestly — slow pushes, lateral drifts — versus the ones it fakes badly (orbits past ~30°, spins through solid sets).
- Light as story: the scene's fire/ice split; candlelit interiors; the unused lane — bright green summer daylight.

### B. Editing rhythm
- Build the grid first: song BPM → beat map → cut points, before touching a frame (Design Bible ch. 5).
- Cut placement: on the beat, against the beat, and the held shot that makes the next cut land harder.
- Transition craft: speed-ramp + dissolve-with-overlay; phase-matched hard cuts across angle changes; when a dissolve is a lie (it ghosts whatever it touches) and a cut is the truth.
- The chorus rule from the field: face and chorus inside 3 seconds; no intros.

### C. Colour & finish
- Unifying takes from different generations into one world: matching temperature, contrast, and grain across cuts (Design Bible ch. 7.2).
- The fire-amber / cold-blue palette as a deliberate system, not an accident per take.
- Finish checks at delivery size *and* phone size — a grade that only works big is not finished.

### D. Generation-model craft
- Prompt construction: verbatim request → identity line → Context block; what each clause actually buys.
- Take strategy: generate long, harvest the middle; expect the first and last seconds to be the forge daydreaming.
- Model literacy: keep current with the toolkit chapter — what each model does with crowds, hands, text, and motion; prices and limits recorded honestly, hedges kept.
- The singing-model road (audio-driven generation, e.g. Wan2.2-S2V class on the Z13 when it is ready) — studied now, used when the machine and the proof allow; never promised before it is demonstrated.

### E. Scene field research (ongoing)
- Keep watching the Viking hip hop scene: new acts, new structures, how the best hide the mouth, how thumbnails evolve.
- Watch *outside* the scene too — the classic directors in the Bible — so our grammar is bigger than one niche.
- Every study session ends in notes, not impressions: what was seen, what it teaches, what we will steal or refuse.

## Practice exercises (the tinkering)

Practice pieces are small, disposable, and judged as harshly as finals — that is what makes them safe to learn on.

1. **The 8-second take drill.** One subject, one move, generated long and middle-harvested. Goal: a take with zero physics defects. Repeat until boring — boring means learned.
2. **The join drill.** Two takes, one transition, five ways: dissolve, phase-matched cut, speed-ramp overlay, world-cut, whip. Judge each against the Physics Law; log the verdicts.
3. **The stride drill.** Walking footage across an angle change (our v13 wound). Practise phase-matching stride across cuts until the join is invisible at 30 fps strips.
4. **The surgery drill.** Take a defective clip and repair it with length-exact minterpolate surgery; verify frame counts before and after — the repair must be provable, not plausible.
5. **The QC drill.** Deliberately plant (or hunt in old versions) the classic tells — tattoo drift, duplicated faces, melting props, a stopped frame — and time how fast each role finds its own kind. The Warden and the Canon Keeper keep score in the log.
6. **The silent-story drill.** Cut a 30-second story with no performer at all — objects, weather, weaving, ravens — that still lands the lyric's feeling. This is the muscle our narrative lane is built from.
7. **The thumbnail drill.** One song, three thumbnails: fire/ice split, single emotional image, persona-forward. Judged on the field guide's hit-vs-flood evidence.

## How learning gets recorded

- **Every study session and every drill** gets an entry in `LEARNING_LOG.md` the same day: what was studied or attempted, what was learned, what changes in our practice.
- **Every finished video** gets a post-mortem entry: defects found, defects fixed, defects confessed — the v13 pattern.
- **Research that outgrows a log line** becomes a note in `~/workspace/research_notes/` and a section in the Music Video Design Bible space, hedges and all.
- **A lesson that proves itself twice becomes a law**: it moves from the log into `CREW_CHARTER.md`'s standing laws, with its date and origin named — the way the dissolve-vs-cut lesson and the unique-material law already have.
- The Scholar reviews the log monthly and updates this program: topics struck through when mastered, new weaknesses added without shame. A weakness written down is a lesson half-learned; a weakness hidden is a defect shipped later.

## The spirit of it

Practise like the forge is a instrument we are learning to play, because it is. Make small things, judge them honestly, keep the failures, steal from the best, and write it all down. The twelve songs ahead are not just deliverables — they are the crew's training ground, each one a chance to be visibly better than the one before.
