# Synthesis 4 — The Gauntlet · Cross-Family Synthesis

Great Research Campaign · Wave 2 · 2026-10-22 · Doctrine: Volmarr's Cyber-Viking Master Visual Design Lawbook

*The Gauntlet is the one protocol any family runs on any deliverable — a screen, a banner, a port, a system — before it may be called done. It turns the Lawbook's acceptance battery, Book XIV's removal and state tests, and the Master Screen Review into a single ordered course with pass and fail at every station. The protocol is subtractive by design: first we take things away and watch whether the work still stands, then we load it with the content and the failures real life will load it with, then we name its defects in the doctrine's own vocabulary, then we close. Beauty is judged last, on purpose — because a hall that only survives its own screenshot was never a hall at all.*

---

## The Shape of the Gauntlet

The Gauntlet has **six stages, run in order, and no stage may be skipped or reordered.** Stages 1–3 test the deliverable; Stage 4 names what failed; Stage 5 closes; Stage 6 is the final word that either confirms the passage or sends it back. The maker never grades her own work — a reviewing family runs the Gauntlet, per the standing protocol in the family charter, and **Tove, Accessibility & Usability Warden of Family II, holds the ship-gate veto under Book XIV**: her veto, once exercised, stands until the defect is repaired and the failed station is re-run and passed. Volmarr's word is the only appeal.

**Universal pass/fail rule, applying at every station:** a station is **PASS** only when its criterion is demonstrated — shown on the actual deliverable, with real (or realistic) content, in the condition the station specifies. A station is **FAIL** when the criterion is asserted but not demonstrated, demonstrated only in the ideal state, or demonstrated by the maker's testimony alone. "It worked on the maker's machine" is a FAIL.

## Stage 1 — The Universal Acceptance Battery

Ten items, taken from §2 of the doctrine. Each item is a station; the deliverable must pass all ten to leave Stage 1.

1. **Three-second recognition.** *Criterion:* a first-time viewer identifies the primary task and the primary action within roughly three seconds. *Fail:* the viewer must hunt, the dominant action is one of several equals, or the task is described in themed language that must be decoded first.
2. **Theme with runes removed.** *Criterion:* with all runes and rune-adjacent symbols stripped, the deliverable still reads as Cyber-Viking — through structure, geometry, material, and composition. *Fail:* remove the runes and a generic app remains. This is theme substitution caught in the act.
3. **Hierarchy with glow and background art disabled.** *Criterion:* with glow off and background art removed, the hierarchy of task, state, and action still reads correctly. *Fail:* the user cannot tell what is primary, active, or actionable without the effects. Paint is not hierarchy.
4. **Keyboard and pointer equivalents.** *Criterion:* every interactive state is reachable and operable by keyboard as well as by pointer, with focus visible in bright and dark contexts. *Fail:* any action that exists only for one input method, or a focus indicator that vanishes on any surface the theme provides.
5. **Long content does not break layout.** *Criterion:* realistic long labels, long names, long messages, and real data volumes keep the layout intact — no overflow, no collision, no clipped essential content. *Fail:* the deliverable was only ever tested with short, heroic placeholder content.
6. **Color never the only status indicator.** *Criterion:* every status, presence, error, and selection signal carried by colour is also carried by label, shape, icon, position, or text. *Fail:* any meaning a colour-blind member, a grayscale print, or a screen reader would miss entirely.
7. **Compact and expanded viewports.** *Criterion:* the deliverable works in at least one compact and one expanded viewport, with reflow that loses no content and no function. *Fail:* a design proven only at the maker's preferred width.
8. **Decorative assets never intercept.** *Criterion:* no decorative asset intercepts pointer input, and no decorative asset enters screen-reader order; reading order is the content's order. *Fail:* an ornament that swallows a click, or a banner a screen reader announces as content.
9. **Animation reducible without losing meaning.** *Criterion:* with motion reduced, every state, change, and signal the animation carried is still conveyed — reduced motion is a *complete alternate state*, not a dimming. *Fail:* a reduced-motion version that merely slows or fades the same motion, or that drops a state signal.
10. **No comfort ornament.** *Criterion:* the reviewer can interrogate every detail for its job — orientation, state, affordance, or change — and every detail answers. *Fail:* any detail that exists solely because an empty corner felt uncomfortable. The answer "it looked empty" is a confession, not a defence.

**Stage 1 verdict:** ten stations, ten passes, or the deliverable returns to its maker with the failed stations named. There is no partial Stage 1.

## Stage 2 — The Removal Tests (Book XIV)

Five removal tests, named in Book XIV's chapter map, plus the Distance Test. The method is subtractive: remove one layer at a time and demand that the work still functions, still reads, still *is itself*.

- **Three-Second Silhouette Test** (Ch. 216). At a glance — silhouette only, no fine detail — the deliverable's primary task and dominant form are recognisable in roughly three seconds. *Pass:* the silhouette alone carries the task. *Fail:* recognition requires reading, zooming, or explanation.
- **Grayscale Test** (Ch. 217). Colour removed entirely. *Pass:* hierarchy, state, and meaning survive; nothing depended on hue alone. *Fail:* any signal, distinction, or emphasis that disappears with colour.
- **No-Rune Test** (Ch. 218). All runes removed. *Pass:* the Cyber-Viking identity persists in structure — longhouse composition, chamfer, frame discipline, material — per the Prime Directive. *Fail:* the theme was the runes.
- **No-Glow Test** (Ch. 219). All glow and atmospheric effects disabled. *Pass:* hierarchy, focus, and state remain legible and ordered. *Fail:* glow was load-bearing — it *was* the hierarchy.
- **No-Art Test** (Ch. 220). All background art and imagery removed. *Pass:* the surface still composes, still orients, still invites. *Fail:* art was masking a broken layout, an empty centre, or a missing focal anchor.
- **Distance Test** (Ch. 221). Viewed at distance — across the room, at arm's length on a wall display, squinted. *Pass:* the dominant anchor, the primary action, and the state of the surface are still readable. *Fail:* meaning that exists only at reading distance on a surface meant to be glanced at, or diagrams whose detail-drunk complexity collapses into noise (Sólrún's standing edge).

**Stage 2 verdict:** six removals, six survivals. A deliverable that needs any removed layer to *function* has failed that layer's test, however beautiful it is with the layer restored.

## Stage 3 — The State Tests

Book XIV's stress discipline: the deliverable is judged not in its portrait but in its life.

- **Long-Content Test** (Ch. 222). Load the deliverable with realistic maximum content: the longest names, the longest messages, the fullest member list, the deepest thread, real data at real volume. *Pass:* layout, reading width, chunking, and scroll behaviour hold; no essential content is clipped or overlapped. *Fail:* any breakage that Stage 1, item 5, should have caught and did not — the station is re-run, not excused.
- **Empty-State Test** (Ch. 223). Load the deliverable with nothing: the new hall, the empty board, the first-run member, the search with no results. *Pass:* the empty state is designed — it orients, it names the next action, it does not pretend to be full or apologise in themed riddle. *Fail:* a blank, a spinner with no exit, or scaffolding shown as content.
- **Failure-State Test** (Ch. 224). Break the deliverable on purpose: offline, server error, failed send, denied permission, agent failure and recovery (Book VIII, Ch. 126). *Pass:* the failure is a designed surface — plain-language explanation, visible state, a recovery path, no loss of the member's work — and recovery restores state honestly. *Fail:* a dead end, a silent swallow, a fake success, or an error written as atmosphere instead of information.

The sequence then continues through Book XIV's remaining stress stations — the **Responsive Stress Test** (Ch. 225), the **Accessibility Gate** (Ch. 226, Tove's home ground: WCAG AA, keyboard traversal, screen-reader semantics, text scaling 125–200%), and the **Performance Gate** (Ch. 227: budgets are per-project, per the doctrine's honesty about what the Lawbook does not specify — but a gate with no budget is not a gate, so the project must name its budgets before this station can be passed).

**Stage 3 verdict:** a deliverable proven only in its ideal state has not been proven. Long, empty, and broken are states every real hall visits daily; the Gauntlet visits them first.

## Stage 4 — Naming the Failures

Defects found in Stages 1–3 are named in the doctrine's own vocabulary (§2: the five failure modes), because a named defect can be assigned, repaired, and re-tested, while a vague unease cannot.

1. **Theme substitution** — runes, neon, or symbols doing the work structure should do. Caught by: No-Rune, battery item 2.
2. **Screenshot bias** — beautiful empty, collapses with real content. Caught by: Long-Content, battery item 5.
3. **Hierarchy inversion** — ornament brighter, louder, or more luminous than the task. Caught by: No-Glow, No-Art, Grayscale, battery item 3. The reduction order applies: reduce ornament, glow, texture, motion, or symbolic density *before* touching information architecture.
4. **Inconsistent physics** — borders, light, motion, or depth behaving differently across surfaces without a semantic reason. Caught by: the Consistency Audit (Stage 5) and by any reviewer who finds the same component behaving two ways.
5. **Accessibility debt** — a cue that depends on colour alone, tiny text, continuous motion, or low contrast. Caught by: Grayscale, battery items 4, 6, 9, the Accessibility Gate. Debt is the operative word: it compounds, and Tove collects.

**Stage 4 rule:** every defect is recorded with its station, its failure-mode name, its severity, and its owner. A defect recorded without a name is sent back to be named.

## Stage 5 — The Closing Sequence (Book XIV)

Run in this order, each step signed by the reviewing family:

1. **Consistency Audit** (Ch. 228) — the deliverable's components, geometry, motion, and material behave as the same system behaves everywhere else in the hall; no improvised corner language, no one-off physics.
2. **Theme Coherence Audit** (Ch. 229) — the deliverable belongs to the civilisation: identity in structure first, symbols verified (Rúnhildr's rune check), budgets held (accent 5–12%, ornament 1–5%, at most three frame strengths, corner cut 6–12 px).
3. **Screenshot Review** (Ch. 230) — the deliverable is reviewed as it will actually be seen, in stills, at compact and expanded sizes — and the screenshots are taken *after* Stages 1–4, never instead of them. A screenshot review that precedes the state tests is screenshot bias with a stamp on it.
4. **Real-Use Review** (Ch. 231) — the deliverable is used, by a reviewer who did not make it, for its real task, with real content, under Tome 01's evaluation discipline and Tome 09's population discipline: beyond the rested, sighted, two-handed, desktop case — keyboard-only, screen-reader, 200% zoom, reduced motion, grayscale, small viewport, long content. Findings are rated by severity (frequency, impact, persistence), not waived as edge cases.
5. **10/10 Screen Score** (Ch. 232) — the deliverable is scored against the project's required quality bar. The Lawbook names no scoring rubric (the doctrine is explicit about this gap); the project must therefore state its rubric before scoring, and a score asserted without a rubric is a FAIL of this step.
6. **Release Checklist** (Ch. 233) — the final enumerated check: stations passed, defects named and closed, tokens verified, alt-and-data law satisfied for every image, budgets named for the Performance Gate, the reviewing family's sign-off recorded.

## Stage 6 — The Ship Gate and the Master Screen Review

**The ship gate is one sentence: no critical unresolved visual defects.** A critical defect is any Stage 1–3 failure that blocks the primary task, breaks meaning in a coherence condition (grayscale, 125–200% scaling, long content, effects off), or creates accessibility debt on an essential signal. Non-critical defects may be recorded and scheduled; critical defects stop the ship. **Tove's veto is the gate's enforcement:** when she exercises it on an accessibility or human-limits defect, the veto stands until the defect is repaired and its station re-run and passed. No deadline, wave, or launch date outranks it; Volmarr's word is the only appeal.

The final word is the **Master Screen Review (Appendix C)**, quoted verbatim in the doctrine and binding on every passage: *"A screen ships only when hierarchy, layout, type, spacing, theme, accessibility, interaction clarity, performance, responsiveness, and originality each meet the project's required quality bar. If an effect cannot justify its informational or emotional job, remove it. The target is forged coherence: breathtaking at first glance, effortless after the thousandth use."* A deliverable that has passed every station but fails this paragraph — effortful on the thousandth use, however breathtaking on the first — does not ship. The thousandth use is the test the screenshot cannot take.

## A Worked Example — The Banner That Failed, Then Passed

A hypothetical deliverable, invented for this volume and labelled as such: **a new banner panel for a Samtal board's landing**, carrying the board's name, a knotwork border, a glowing rune emblem, and a one-line description in display type over a cinematic background image.

**First passage — FAIL.** Stage 1, item 1: a first-time viewer takes seven seconds to find the board's name — the glowing emblem outshines the title (a defect is already visible). Item 3: with glow and art disabled, the description text vanishes entirely — it was set *in* the image, not over it. Item 6: the board's "active" state is a gold border alone, no label. Item 8: the banner image is announced by a screen reader as content, though it is decorative-plus-title. Stage 2: the **No-Rune Test fails** — remove the emblem and the panel is a generic rounded rectangle; nothing structural remains. The **No-Glow Test fails** — glow was the hierarchy. The **Grayscale Test fails** — the gold "active" border is indistinguishable from the silver "quiet" border. Stage 3: the **Long-Content Test fails** — a board with a long real name overflows the panel and clips the description. Stage 4 names the defects: hierarchy inversion (emblem over title), theme substitution (runes as identity), accessibility debt (colour-only state, image-borne text), screenshot bias (proven only with a short name). **Tove exercises the veto at the Accessibility Gate.** The banner does not ship.

**Repair.** The maker re-seats the work under the reduction order — ornament first, architecture untouched. The board name moves into real interface type at display scale over the panel's own dark ground; the image becomes background art in an identity zone, excluded from screen-reader order, with its genuine alt description embedded as data in the file. The emblem shrinks to a verified semantic mark at a corner node; knotwork becomes a structural border (one of the panel's three frame strengths, 6–12 px chamfer). "Active" gains a text label and a shape change beside its colour. Accent coverage is measured and brought inside 5–12%; glow is rebuilt as core + halo + atmosphere on the primary action only.

**Second passage — PASS.** Stage 1: the name and the join action are found in under three seconds; hierarchy survives glow-off; long names wrap inside the panel without clipping; keyboard focus is visible on the dark ground and over the image edge. Stage 2: with runes removed, the chamfered panel, knotwork border, and longhouse composition still read as the civilisation; grayscale keeps active and quiet boards distinct by label and shape; at distance, the board name and its state still read. Stage 3: the empty board shows a designed empty state naming its first action; a failed image load leaves a composed panel on the darkness ladder, not a hole. Stage 5 closes in order; the 10/10 score is given against the project's stated rubric; the Release Checklist records Tove's re-test and sign-off. **The banner ships** — not because it became more beautiful (though it did), but because it survived having everything taken away.

## The Protocol in One Page

1. Seat a reviewing family that did not make the work; Tove holds the veto; Volmarr's word is the only appeal.
2. Run the ten-item acceptance battery, in order, on the real deliverable with real content; ten passes or return.
3. Run the six removal tests — Three-Second Silhouette, Grayscale, No-Rune, No-Glow, No-Art, Distance; the work must survive every removal.
4. Run the state tests — Long-Content, Empty-State, Failure-State — then Responsive Stress, the Accessibility Gate (WCAG AA), and the Performance Gate against budgets the project has named.
5. Name every defect by its failure mode — theme substitution, screenshot bias, hierarchy inversion, inconsistent physics, accessibility debt — with station, severity, and owner.
6. Apply the reduction order to every theme-versus-task conflict: ornament, glow, texture, motion, symbolic density first; information architecture last.
7. Close in order: Consistency Audit → Theme Coherence Audit → Screenshot Review → Real-Use Review (beyond the rested, sighted, desktop case) → 10/10 Screen Score (against a stated rubric) → Release Checklist.
8. Hold the ship gate: no critical unresolved visual defects ship; Tove's veto stands until repair and re-test.
9. Speak the Master Screen Review as the final word: every dimension at the project's bar, every effect justifying its job, forged coherence — breathtaking at first glance, effortless after the thousandth use.
10. Record the passage: stations, defects, repairs, sign-offs. A passage that is not recorded did not happen.
