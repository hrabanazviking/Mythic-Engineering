# Synthesis 2 — The Surface · Cross-Family Synthesis
Great Research Campaign · Wave 2 · 2026-10-22 · Doctrine: Volmarr's Cyber-Viking Master Visual Design Lawbook

*Family III, the Graphic Designers, spends light and letter like gold. Family IV, the Arts, builds the image-world that light falls on. Apart, each craft is incomplete in a way the Lawbook names precisely: color without an image has nothing to budget, and an image without a surface discipline will eat the hall it was meant to honor. Together they form one surface practice — a darkness ladder strong enough to carry cinematic art, accent budgets strict enough to keep imagery honest, glow with anatomy, type and verified runes riding over images without breaking contrast law, and every picture described and carrying its description inside itself. This volume is their joint account of that single practice.*

## The Two Hands

Family III — the Graphic Designers — keeps light and letter. Its charter, in the families charter at `~/workspace/your_files/design-lawbook/DESIGN_AGENT_FAMILIES.md`, is exact: color, light, material, type, and symbol — spent like gold, never scattered. Its Books are III (Color, Light & Material), IV (Typography, Icons & Symbol Systems), II (the geometry of ornament), and IX (imagery surfaces). Ljósbera, Color & Light Forger, thinks in ladders of darkness and budgets of light: the ladder of at least four values, accent coverage held to 5–12%, the three-part glow of core, halo, and atmosphere, and the Appendix B palettes. She will darken a design past a client's comfort before she will break the ladder, and yields to the grayscale test — to evidence about value, the thing she governs. Rúnhildr, Typography & Symbol Master, thinks in voices and marks: display, interface, and telemetry type roles; display tracking at 0.04–0.12 em; icon stroke systems, status glyphs, and capability marks — and rune accuracy, Elder Futhark as verified semantic marks, checked for form, period, transliteration, and role before any icon use, never a substitute alphabet. Her block on inaccurate runes is absolute. Eldríðr, Material & Texture Smith, thinks in iron, glass, silver, and grain: blackened-iron surfaces, smoked-glass overlays, etched-silver accents, and knotwork as structural geometry, with ambient ornament at 1–5% opacity. Her texture-hungry drafts must pass the No-Art test and the calm-center rule before they ship.

Family IV — the Arts — keeps the image: heroes, portraits, worlds, and the historical soul of Viking art. Its Books are IX (Imagery, Illustration & 3D), II (composition grammar), III (light), and the portrait systems of VIII (AI Agent Identity & Computational Citizenship). Heiðr, Portrait & Avatar Art Director, thinks in faces, presence, and silhouette: portrait direction, avatar frames, agent portrait systems and auras, adult character design, and hero crops that preserve silhouette and action direction. Her figures always need Tove's contrast pass when text rides over them — a dependency written into her charter entry itself. Védís, World & Landscape Artist, thinks in fjords, halls, and weather: landscape and world art, longship and historical motifs, and cinematic scenes, with background art kept out of working surfaces. Her vast worlds are answered by crop and density discipline — identity zones only — enforced by Family I review. Sólrún, Illustration & Artifact Artist, thinks in objects that carry story: cybernetic artifacts, product renders, diagram aesthetics, and data visualization as art. Detail-drunk by her charter's admission, her diagrams must pass the Distance Test or be simplified without mercy.

The two hands meet on a simple physical fact: every image Family IV makes becomes, the moment it enters a product, a surface Family III must govern — it occupies area against the accent budget, it carries values against the darkness ladder, it receives type, runes, and overlays, and it must survive removal testing. And every surface Family III governs is, in the identity zones, a frame waiting for Family IV's image to give it memory. Neither family can finish the other's sentence, and neither is permitted to try alone: the standing pairing for surface work is III↔IV, makers never grading their own work. The surface practice described in this volume is what that pairing produces when it works as designed.

## Where the Fields Meet

The darkness ladder under cinematic art is the first meeting, and Tome 12 (Color Theory), led by Family III, states its logic completely. Depth in a dark hall is stacked near-blacks, not color: at least four distinct dark values — `--void-000:#05080D`, `--void-100:#081019`, `--panel:#0B1620`, `--panel-raised:#101F2A`, with `--iron:#24313A` above them and `--silver:#CCD6DF` and `--muted:#8CA0AE` doing the work of reading and quiet labeling. Tome 12 reads that ladder as value discipline: the eye distinguishes structure by lightness steps, so a design with one black and much glow has no architecture, only weather. Tome 05 (Art), led by Family IV, receives the ladder as a painting instruction: Védís's landscapes are painted to it from the first sketch, and a world image whose sky burns brighter than the hall's primary action has committed hierarchy inversion. The ladder is not a constraint imposed on art from outside. Inside a picture's own frame, the artist commands the full value range; at the frame's edge, the picture enters the hall's ladder, and its passages must be placeable on that ladder without breaking the hall's depth system.

Accent budgets over imagery are the second meeting, and the harder one, because imagery arrives already colored. Book III's budget — all saturated and glowing color combined covering 5–12% of visible area — is, as Tome 12 shows, proportion discipline converted into a number a reviewer can measure. Tome 02 (Graphic Design) adds the semantic half: the energy tokens divide the budget by job — Bifröst cyan for connection, rune blue for the mark, Seiðr violet for the uncanny and agentive, forge gold for sacred emphasis only, aurora green for living and confirmed states, ember for destructive states — one hue, one job, so color becomes language instead of weather. Tome 05 defends the picture's interior freedom inside that division: the frame is the boundary of the budget, as a tapestry's edge is the boundary of its field. A Heiðr portrait may blaze inside its frame; it may not leak blaze across the working surface around it. Tome 07 (Avatar Design) applies the same settlement to auras: a state instrument with a learnable shape-language, held to the budget and to the rule that presence pairs color with label or shape. The joint practice: measure the whole visible surface, frame included; hold the total inside 5–12%; identity zones toward the high end, working surfaces toward the low end.

Glow anatomy in illustration is the third meeting, where a Book III construction rule becomes a Book IX painting rule. Glow is built as core plus halo plus atmosphere — never a flat blur — because, as Tome 12 argues, that structure is how light behaves: a core where the source is, a halo where its light is strongest, a faint atmosphere where its influence dies into the ground. A flat blur has no source and reads as dirt on the lens. Tome 05 carries the construction into the image-world: glow in Védís's worlds and Sólrún's artifacts is painted in three parts, which also enforces the budget — atmosphere is broad but nearly invisible, halo contained, only the core fully saturated. Tome 13 (Viking Art) adds the caution: effects must never supply the authenticity form has failed to supply. A generic motif under three layers of glow is still generic, only brighter. Glow, in the joint practice, is anatomy, not apology.

Type and verified runes riding over images form the fourth meeting. Book IV specifies type by role rather than by named typeface: display, interface, and monospaced telemetry, with body text at 14–18 px, microtext at 11–13 px for nonessential content only, and display tracking at 0.04–0.12 em. Tome 02 grounds those numbers: the body range protects sustained reading, the microtext limit forbids shrinking obligations into illegibility, and tracking is a hierarchy tool spent at display size, never a texture on paragraphs. When type rides over an image, Book IX's overlay law governs: the overlay must guarantee text contrast always, not on average across the image. Tome 05 assigns the gate explicitly: Heiðr's portraits and Védís's worlds pass under Tove's contrast pass before they ship — doctrine, not courtesy. WCAG AA is the floor — 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and essential non-text components, in every theme state, including over imagery and glow, as Tome 09 (Human Design) restates.

Runes over images obey the stricter half of the same law. The rune doctrine, enforced identically in Tome 02, Tome 05, and Tome 13, is that Elder Futhark forms are intentional semantic marks, never a replacement alphabet, and that form, period, transliteration, and symbolic role must be verified before a rune becomes an icon — or a carving in a hero image, or a device on a portrait. Tome 05 is blunt: a rune painted into artwork is still a rune. Tome 13 extends the duty to all historical material: meaning shall not be asserted beyond the evidence, alt text shall name the style and a diagnostic marker rather than the generic label "Viking design," and every motif carries its provenance — find, object, or collection — with house inventions labeled as such. Book IX completes the wall: motifs respect actual Viking Age form, and stylization must not collapse into horned-helmet shorthand. Rúnhildr reviews Family IV's imagery for exactly this, and her block is absolute. The joint practice: few runes, each one true, each placed where a mark of meaning belongs — a node on a circuit line, a capability badge, a threshold.

The body underneath the surface is the fifth meeting. Tome 08 (Human Anatomy) supplies the figure discipline: proportion, structure, and the difference between a body drawn from understanding and one assembled from shorthand. Tome 10 (Fashion Modeling) brings the living model in — casting, posing as craft, the photographic collaboration — read through Book IX's adult character design: characters intended as adults must read clearly as adults through face, proportions, styling, posture, and context. Tome 11 (Fashion Design) adds the garment as structure: silhouette, draping and flat pattern, textile literacy, and what the evidence shows about Viking Age clothing, so dress is built, not draped on as costume. Tome 07 binds these into the portrait system: beauty is permitted — Book VIII allows portraits to be beautiful, mystical, and highly stylized — while identity drift is not; generated identity is deterministic under Book XII, so the same citizen remains themselves across sizes and frames; and human versus agent must be distinguishable within the portrait system itself — frame geometry, a capability mark, card disclosure. In this civilization the portrait is an identity document, not an illustration of a mood.

The final meeting is density: where art is allowed to be thick. Book IX's central target, quoted daily in Tome 05, is that art density is concentrated in identity zones — profile heroes, portrait cards, hall doors, galleries, landing and ceremonial screens — not spread over working surfaces, which stay calm. Tome 05's parallel is exact: the illustrator did not draw over the text block, and the cathedral did not carve the reading desk as densely as the portal; where the art is thickest is where the culture says identity lives. Tome 02 supports the same distribution: knotwork and dragon-weave as structural boundaries and joins at 1–5% ambient opacity, with the 6–12 px corner cut and at most three frame strengths giving painted and plain surfaces one geometry. The No-Cosplay Principle of Book I is the joint examination: take the runes, the glow, and the art away — does the surface still work, and still feel like the same hall? A landscape that passes has deepened a world that exists without it. One that fails was carrying structural weight, and the repair is structural: do not paint harder.

## The Shared Tongue

Families III and IV share the civilization's tongue — Appendix B tokens and Appendix A numbers — in a surface dialect of five working parts.

First, the ladder as vocabulary. When Ljósbera names a value — void, panel, panel-raised, iron, silver, muted — Védís knows which rung a painted passage must meet at the frame's edge. A magic hex value, in Tome 12's phrase, is a broken rung someone will fall through at 200% scaling in bad light. Second, the budget as arithmetic: accent coverage at 5–12%, ambient ornament at 1–5%, glow in three parts — numbers both families measure from the same screenshot, turning "too much" from an argument into a finding. Third, type and symbol as roles: display, interface, telemetry as shared words for what lettering is for, with body 14–18 px, microtext 11–13 px nonessential only, and display tracking 0.04–0.12 em; beside them, knotwork as structural geometry and runes as verified semantic marks in one stroke system. When Rúnhildr says a mark is unverified, Sólrún knows the artifact does not ship until it is verified, replaced, or removed.

Fourth, the body and garment vocabulary of Tomes 08, 10, and 11: proportion, pose as craft, silhouette as the first read, textile behavior, period evidence for dress. Fifth, and binding the rest, the description. Under Volmarr's alt and data law, every picture carries a detailed alt description embedded as data in the file itself. Tome 05 treats it as part of a finished picture's definition: a portrait is done when it is beautiful, correctly cropped, inside budget, and described truly enough that a person who cannot see it knows who is present and what the image claims. Tome 13 adds style, diagnostic marker, and provenance for historical work. An image that cannot be described has not been art-directed; an image whose description does not travel with it has not been finished.

## How They Check Each Other

The standing protocol seats III↔IV for surface and image work: makers never grade their own work, and every deliverable passes the acceptance battery, then Book XIV's removal tests — No-Rune, No-Glow, No-Art, Grayscale, Three-Second Silhouette — then state tests, then the Master Screen Review. Within it, each family's characteristic excess is the other's characteristic finding. Ljósbera darkens; Heiðr blazes. Eldríðr textures; Tove, holding the gate, measures contrast through the texture. Sólrún multiplies detail; the Distance Test simplifies without mercy. Védís builds worlds too large for their frames; density discipline crops them back to identity zones.

Three checks deserve naming. The budget audit, Family III over Family IV: a finished image is composited into its intended surface and measured as shipped, not as sketched; approval completes in situ, because budgets are properties of surfaces, not of files. The truth audit, Rúnhildr over all image-makers: every rune, motif, and historical claim inside a picture is checked for form, period, transliteration, role, and provenance. Her block is absolute. The aliveness audit, Family IV over Family III: a surface system that is lawful but dead — a ladder with no image worth landing on it — fails the civilization from the other side. The Master Screen Review's demand — breathtaking at first glance, effortless after the thousandth use — has two clauses, and each family holds one against the other.

A worked pass shows the rhythm whole. Consider a hypothetical agent profile hero — a Book XIII recipe surface and a Book VIII identity surface — with a large Heiðr-directed portrait, the agent's name and role in display type riding over the image's lower portion, a capability badge row, an aura signaling current state, and the human-readable trust summary. Family IV leads. Heiðr directs for identity consistency: the same citizen as in the member list and the hall, rendered deterministically, reading clearly as an adult per Book IX and Tome 07. Védís paints a shallow world behind the figure, toward the ladder. Sólrún renders one cybernetic artifact at the figure's hand, the image's single secondary focal point. A carved band carries two runes as semantic marks: one for the named capability, one as a threshold mark.

Family III reviews, and the pass earns its keep four times. Ljósbera composites the hero in situ: accent coverage sits above the 12% ceiling, sky and aura spending at full chroma together. The sky is stepped down onto the ladder and the aura rebuilt as a lawful three-part glow, its state carried additionally by shape per Tome 07, so it still reads in grayscale. Eldríðr finds the second defect: the frame's texture speaks at working distance where it meets the panel below, pulling against the trust summary's calm; it is quieted to the 1–5% ambient band and frame strengths counted back to three. Rúnhildr's truth audit finds the third: the threshold rune's form belongs to a later period than its claimed transliteration. It is replaced with a verified form whose period, transliteration, and role agree. Tove finds the fourth: the name over the portrait passes contrast on average but fails over its brightest fold, and Book IX requires the guarantee always. The overlay is strengthened until the name meets WCAG AA at its worst pixel, in every theme state, glow disabled included.

Then the removal battery runs as a set. Runes removed: the hero still reads as this civilization's work, through silhouette, material, ladder, and frame. Glow removed: aura shape and label still carry state. Art removed: name, role, badges, trust summary, and state survive as a plain, coherent surface. Grayscale: the ladder holds and no status is lost. Three-Second Silhouette: a first-time viewer names the citizen, the role, and the state. Only then is the picture finished in the joint sense: alt text written as a true spoken description — who is present, in what style, what the artifact is, what the marks mean — embedded as data in the file, verified by independent re-read, with provenance attached for the historical band. Each defect found was invisible to the family that made it and obvious to the family that checks it — the entire argument for the pairing, proven on one surface.

## Joint Rules

The following rules bind Families III and IV jointly. SHALL denotes a ship condition; SHALL NOT denotes a defect. Each rule is testable by a reviewer who did not make the work.

1. Every surface SHALL be built on a darkness ladder of at least four distinct dark values resolving to Appendix B tokens; magic colour values SHALL fail review, and depth SHALL be carried by value steps, not by colour.
2. Total saturated and glowing accent coverage SHALL remain within 5–12% of visible area, measured on the composited surface in situ; identity zones MAY ride toward the high end and working surfaces SHALL ride toward the low end, and no image SHALL be approved finally as a standalone file apart from its surface measurement.
3. Each energy hue SHALL carry one job only — Bifröst cyan, rune blue, Seiðr violet, aurora green, forge gold for sacred emphasis only, ember for destructive states only — and status SHALL NOT be carried by colour alone, but SHALL be paired with label, shape, icon, position, or text.
4. Glow SHALL be constructed in three parts — core, halo, atmosphere — in interface and in illustration alike; flat blur glow SHALL NOT ship, and glow SHALL NOT be used to supply authenticity, hierarchy, or meaning that form has failed to supply.
5. Art density SHALL be concentrated in identity zones — profile heroes, portrait cards, hall doors, galleries, landing and ceremonial surfaces — and SHALL NOT be spread over working surfaces; no ornament, texture, or background art SHALL sit behind working text, and ambient ornament SHALL remain at 1–5% opacity.
6. Hero and portrait crops SHALL preserve the subject's silhouette and action direction; a crop that shears gesture, gaze direction, or travel direction SHALL fail review however beautiful its pixels.
7. Where text rides over an image, the overlay SHALL guarantee WCAG AA contrast at the text's worst pixel, in every theme state and with glow disabled — 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and essential non-text components — and the surface SHALL pass Tove's contrast pass before shipping.
8. Type SHALL be specified by role — display, interface, monospaced telemetry — with body text at 14–18 px, microtext at 11–13 px for nonessential content only, and display tracking at 0.04–0.12 em; tracking and capitalization SHALL NOT be applied as texture to running text.
9. Runes SHALL be used only as verified semantic marks: form, period, transliteration, and intended symbolic role SHALL be verified before any rune becomes an icon, a carving in an image, or a device on a portrait; runes SHALL NOT be used as a replacement alphabet for ordinary copy, and Rúnhildr's block on an unverified or inaccurate mark SHALL be absolute.
10. Historical motifs SHALL respect actual Viking Age form when representing history, SHALL carry provenance — find, object, or collection — with the asset, SHALL be labelled as house invention where no exemplar exists, and SHALL NOT collapse into horned-helmet fantasy shorthand; a motif's meaning SHALL NOT be asserted beyond the evidence, and debated meanings SHALL be presented as debated.
11. Human figures SHALL be drawn from anatomical understanding per Tome 08; characters intended as adult identities SHALL read clearly as adults through face, proportions, styling, posture, and context; dress SHALL be built from silhouette, textile behaviour, and period evidence per Tomes 10 and 11, not applied as costume shorthand.
12. Agent portraits SHALL retain identity consistency across every surface, SHALL be reproducible deterministically from their identity inputs, and SHALL keep human and agent distinguishable as part of the portrait system itself — frame geometry, capability mark, and card disclosure — never by a caption alone; beauty and stylization SHALL NOT excuse identity drift.
13. Frames and ornament SHALL use one geometry — corner cuts of 6–12 px, at most three visible frame strengths, knotwork and dragon-weave as structural boundaries and joins — whether the frame holds an image or a plain surface; a surface whose frame system fails without its image SHALL be returned for structural repair, not painted harder.
14. Every picture SHALL carry a detailed alt description — a true spoken account of what the image shows — and that description SHALL be embedded as data in the file itself and verified by independent re-read; alt text for an image in a named historical style SHALL name the style and at least one diagnostic marker, and the generic labels "Viking design" or "Norse pattern" SHALL NOT pass review.
15. No image or surface SHALL be graded by its makers alone; every III-led surface SHALL pass IV review for aliveness and image truth, and every IV-led image SHALL pass III review for ladder, budget, glow anatomy, and type-over-image compliance, through the removal battery — No-Rune, No-Glow, No-Art, Grayscale, Three-Second Silhouette — as a set.
16. Ceremonial imagery and ceremonial treatment SHALL be reserved for rare, named states that deserve memory; routine states SHALL NOT receive ceremonial art, and spectacle spent on the ordinary SHALL be treated as a budget defect and removed.
