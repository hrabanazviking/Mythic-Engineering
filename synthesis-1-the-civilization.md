# Synthesis 1 — The Civilization · Cross-Family Synthesis
Great Research Campaign · Wave 2 · 2026-10-22 · Doctrine: Volmarr's Cyber-Viking Master Visual Design Lawbook

*Two families keep one civilization from opposite ends of the same beam. Family I, the Designers, keeps the civilization coherent — its doctrine, its tokens, its composition, its recipes — so that a hundred screens built by different hands still read as one hall. Family II, the UX Designers, keeps the task sacred inside that hall — its flows, its conversations, its moments of touch and failure — so that coherence never becomes an excuse for a journey that fails the person walking it. This volume is their joint account: architecture before ornament and the task is sacred are not two slogans in tension. They are one practice, stated twice, and neither half survives alone.*

## The Two Hands

Family I — the Designers — guards the system. Its charter, set out in the families charter at `~/workspace/your_files/design-lawbook/DESIGN_AGENT_FAMILIES.md`, is civilization-keeping: hold the whole design civilization coherent across every hall Volmarr builds. Its Books are I (Doctrine of the Digital North), II (Visual Grammar & Geometry), V (Grid, Layout & Composition), XII (Design System Engineering), and XIII (Pattern Library & Screen Recipes), with XIV (Production QA & Design Law Enforcement) as its enforcement arm. Its three keepers embody three altitudes of that work. Hallveig, Master Designer and Keeper of the Doctrine, thinks in systems and civilizations, never in screens: design-system architecture, token governance with no magic values, theme coherence audits, and the Master Screen Review. Rannveig, Composition Architect, thinks in longhouses and ships: axis, ribs, calm centers, the 12-column desktop grid and 16-column ultrawide grid, focal anchors, the Three-Border Limit, and Primary Workspace Calm. Solveig, Design Engineer of the Forge, thinks in contracts between design and code: component-forge governance under Book VI, design-to-code contracts, asset naming, versioning and migration, deterministic identity generation, and visual regression. Together they answer a single question before any other: does this belong to the same civilization as everything else we have built, and can we prove it without relying on its costume?

Family II — the UX Designers — guards the task. Its charter is stated without softening: the task is sacred; ornament never eats it. Its Books are VII (Communication, Community & Social UX), XI (Accessibility, Performance & Resilience), X (Motion, Sound, Haptics & Spatial UX), the recipes of XIII seen from the walker's side, and the gates of XIV. Its three keepers divide the journey itself. Hrafnhildr, UX Architect of Halls and Flows, thinks in journeys — doors, halls, thresholds — and owns conversation shells, message anatomy, threads, composer design, hall and channel navigation, onboarding and first-run flows, and the Samtal Hall recipe (Book XIII, Chapter 196). Ingvild, Interaction & Motion Designer, thinks in cause and effect, in milliseconds: state visibility, hover and press and selection behavior, the named motions, loading and notification behavior, UI sound and haptics. Tove, Accessibility & Usability Warden, thinks like the thousandth user, on the worst device, in the worst light: WCAG AA gates, complete reduced-motion alternate states, color-independent meaning, screen-reader semantics, text scaling at 125–200%, long-content and empty and failure states, and real-use reviews. She holds the veto under Book XIV's ship gate, and she uses it.

The division is not system versus user. Tome 09 (Human Design), led by Family II, is explicit that human limits — anthropometrics, perception, working memory, error — are specifications, not sentiments; Tome 04 (Product Design), led by Family I, is equally explicit that a product is hired for a job in the user's own language, and a feature with no traceable job shall not be built. Family I guards against drift — local decisions accumulating until the civilization is no longer one civilization. Family II guards against capture — the moment a beautiful system or ceremonial flourish consumes the attention the task needed. A civilization that drifts cannot be learned; a journey that is captured cannot be completed. Both failures are civilization failures.

## Where the Fields Meet

The woven practice begins where the two families' lead tomes converge on the same sentence from different evidence. Tome 01 (UX Design) reads the Lawbook's Universal Law — every themed element must have a measurable job: help the user recognize where they are, what is active, what can be acted upon, or what changed — as mainstream UX restated as oath: orientation, state, affordance, feedback. Tome 03 (Visual Design), led by Family I, reaches the same law from perception: hierarchy is the ordering of attention, Gestalt grouping and information discipline do the work, and an element that performs none of those jobs taxes choice and attention while pretending to be neutral. When two independent fields, one starting from the eye and one starting from the journey, arrive at the same removal rule, the rule stops being a taste and becomes the joint practice's foundation: an element with no job is removed, and the argument about removing it is over before it starts.

Token governance serving flows is the first concrete weave. Book XII requires that all design values resolve to named semantic tokens — the seven token families, including Depth Tokens and Ornament Tokens — with no magic values in product components, and versioning and migration for token changes, enforced through Solveig's design-to-code contracts. Tome 04 makes this a product rule: magic values fail review, and token changes never ship as silent local edits. Family II's interest is not tidiness. A flow is a promise repeated across dozens of surfaces: this color means this state, this spacing means this grouping, this motion means this change. If a single screen invents its own values, the promise breaks where the user has learned to trust it, and Hrafnhildr's journey — which depends on recognition carried from door to hall to composer without relearning — breaks with it. Conversely, when Tove shows a token pair failing WCAG AA contrast in a real state, the token set itself must change, through governance, not around it. Governance is how flows stay learnable; flows are how governance stays honest.

Composition serving journeys is the second weave, and Tome 06 (Architecture) states it most fully. Every hall declares one dominant focal centre, every other element describable by its graduated distance from it; every hall is classifiable as Longhouse Composition — dwelling, lingering, gathering — or Ship-Built Composition — directed travel toward a goal. Circulation is audited before styling: the reviewer traces the three most frequent journeys, and any journey requiring backtracking through a working area is redesigned. Tome 03 carries the same discipline into visual grammar: Book V's furniture vocabulary — left navigation rail, right context rail, top mast, bottom command bar — is one vocabulary for user and developer surfaces alike, and Primary Workspace Calm keeps the center quiet. Family II receives this as the precondition of its own work: a conversation shell (Book VII) can only be calm if the composition has already reserved a calm center for it. Tome 06's Lynch audit makes the joint test explicit: paths, edges, districts, nodes, and at least one landmark must be nameable by a reviewer who did not design the hall, after a single traversal. Rannveig cannot pass it alone, because naming requires a walker; Hrafnhildr cannot pass it alone, because walking requires a place.

The hearth sentence is where the weave becomes visible in a single surface. Book VII, Chapter 97, carries the Lawbook's bespoke line, preserved in the doctrine distillation: "The message stream is the hearth. Strong theme belongs mostly in the shell around it." Tome 01 treats this as the governing UX rule for halls: message width optimized for reading rather than full-bleed display, secondary controls revealed on hover or focus where appropriate, presence always paired — color with label or shape. Tome 06 treats the same sentence as longhouse composition: the message column is the central nave where attention gathers, navigation is the side aisle and bench row kept at the edge, and profile heroes, the join door, and identity panels are the carved portals where splendor is permitted and expected. Tome 09 adds the human specification underneath both readings: sustained-attention surfaces must present state changes by redundant, summarized signals rather than raw event floods, and current state must remain perceivable without interrogation. Three tomes, two families, one surface, no contradiction — because the sentence was always both a composition rule and a task rule, and it took both families to read it whole.

Product discipline binds the weave at the level of what gets built at all. Tome 04's Jobs-to-be-Done reading — every product and major surface names, in writing, the job it is hired for, in the user's language, with its circumstance and criteria of progress, before form work begins — is Family I stating a system rule that only Family II's methods can satisfy: discovery evidence must come from real use, observation in context, and shipped-product behavior, not from artefacts without evidence. Tome 04's reduction order for shrinking scope — ornament first, then ceremony, then features, with the job, the information architecture, and accessibility reduced last and only with the loss recorded — is the Universal Law's reduction order extended from the screen to the roadmap, and both families enforce it together: Family I because architecture is civilization, Family II because the task is sacred. Tome 09 completes the triangle by treating workload itself as a budget: any addition of motion, sound, badges, notifications, or ambient theatre must name the attention it costs and the element removed or quieted to pay for it. A civilization that budgets its tokens but not its attention will still bankrupt its users; a task practice that budgets attention but not tokens will still drift into illegibility. The joint practice budgets both.

Finally, the two families meet in failure. Book XI insists that offline, degraded, and error-recovery states are first-class design surfaces. Tome 09's error taxonomy — for every consequential action, name the foreseeable slip, lapse, and mistake, and provide a constraint against the slip, visible state against the lapse, and a plain-language recovery path — is only implementable if Book XIII's recipe system has already made room for those states as named patterns rather than apologies. Tome 01 and Tome 03 converge here: a design proven only on ideal content is recorded as unproven. The bad day is where system and task are the same thing: a person, under stress, trying to recover, inside a structure that either holds or does not.

## The Shared Tongue

The two families speak a common tongue of tokens, numbers, and laws, and the tongue is not metaphorical: it is a literal shared vocabulary without which mutual review would be two monologues.

The tokens are Appendix B of the Lawbook, preserved verbatim in the doctrine distillation, and governed under Book XII. The darkness values — `--void-000:#05080D`, `--void-100:#081019`, `--panel:#0B1620`, `--panel-raised:#101F2A`, `--iron:#24313A`, `--silver:#CCD6DF`, `--muted:#8CA0AE` — give both families the same words for depth, elevation, and disabled state. The energy tokens — `--bifrost:#42DDF5`, `--rune:#5AA8FF`, `--seidr:#9B70FF`, `--forge:#E3B75B`, `--aurora:#59DFAE`, `--ember:#F36A62` — give them the same words for meaning, each hue bound to one job. The spacing scale (`--space-1:4px` through `--space-9:96px`) and motion tokens (`--motion-fast:100ms`, `--motion-standard:180ms`, `--motion-structural:320ms`, `--motion-ceremonial:600ms`) complete the set. When Hallveig says "no magic values," she means these tokens are the only permitted values; when Ingvild specifies a transition, she spends from the same motion tokens rather than inventing a duration. The shared tongue is what makes a review finding portable: "this surface uses a non-token value" is a complete, verifiable sentence in both families' mouths.

The numbers are Appendix A and the Books' hard figures, binding on both families in full. Spacing base 4 px; compact control height 32 px, confined to dense pointer-led expert surfaces; standard control height 40–48 px; minimum touch target 44×44 px; panel padding 24–32 px; section separation 48–96 px; rails collapsed 60–72 px, expanded 220–288 px; context rail 300–420 px. Corner cut 6–12 px; at most three frame strengths; ambient ornament 1–5%; accent coverage 5–12%; at least four dark surface values. Body text 14–18 px; microtext 11–13 px, nonessential only; display tracking 0.04–0.12 em. Motion: micro 80–140 ms, standard 160–220 ms, structural 260–360 ms, ceremonial 450–800 ms and rare. Contrast at WCAG AA or better; scaling coherent at 125–200%. These numbers appear across Tomes 01, 03, 04, 06, and 09 with the same status: floors and ceilings a reviewer can measure. The five named failure modes — theme substitution, screenshot bias, hierarchy inversion, inconsistent physics, accessibility debt — are likewise shared vocabulary.

The laws are the Universal Law, the reduction order, the coherence conditions, and the acceptance battery. The reduction order — reduce ornament, glow, texture, motion, or symbolic density before changing the information architecture — decides every conflict between system beauty and task survival in advance. The coherence conditions — grayscale, 125–200% scaling, long real content, effects disabled — define where both families' work must still function. The acceptance battery's ten tests and Book XIV's removal tests — No-Rune, No-Glow, No-Art, Grayscale, Three-Second Silhouette — followed by state tests, Consistency and Theme Coherence Audits, Screenshot and Real-Use Reviews, the 10/10 Screen Score, and the Release Checklist, with the ship gate of no critical unresolved defects, form the single gauntlet both families run. Neither family maintains a private gate: there is one gate, spoken in one language, and both families stand at it.

## How They Check Each Other

The standing protocol, from the families charter, is plain: a design task seats one lead family and one reviewing family; makers never grade their own work; the standard pairing for system and task work is I↔II; every deliverable passes the acceptance battery, then Book XIV's removal tests, then state tests, then the Master Screen Review. Inside that protocol, four checks carry the I↔II relationship, and each has a named keeper.

Tove's ship-gate veto is the hardest check. Under Book XIV, no surface ships with a critical unresolved visual or human-limits defect, and Tove holds the veto on exactly those defects: contrast, target size, scaling, motion accommodation, color-only meaning, memory demand. Tome 09 states the rule without remainder: Tove's veto on such a defect stands until repair and re-test. The veto is not a preference with seniority; it is a measurement with authority. Family I cannot overrule it with coherence arguments, because coherence at 200% scaling is itself a Family I law — Tove is, in that moment, enforcing Family I's own doctrine against Family I's current draft.

Hallveig's refusal to bless the unproven is the mirror check, running from Family I toward Family II. Hallveig is slow to bless anything unproven and never takes a rush job that must skip the removal tests. Where Tove blocks a surface that fails the human, Hallveig blocks a flow that fails the civilization: a journey that works once, on one screen, by means of values, geometries, or patterns that exist nowhere else in the system and are recorded nowhere in the recipes. Her question — where does this live in Book XIII, under which tokens, at which version — is not bureaucracy. A flow that cannot be named as a recipe cannot be maintained, migrated, or taught, and Hrafnhildr's next hall will silently diverge from it. Hallveig's refusal protects future journeys from present improvisation, just as Tove's veto protects present journeys from present harm.

Hrafnhildr's simplifier's knife is the third check, cutting toward both families. She will cut a beloved feature that fails the three-second test — primary task and primary action obvious within roughly three seconds. A Family I composition presenting two elements at equal maximum intensity, in violation of Tome 06's single-centre rule, will fail that test in practice, and her finding will name the composition, not the copy, as the defect. Volmarr's word is the only appeal against the knife. The pairing itself is the fourth check, and the most ordinary: Solveig's contracts are reviewed by Ingvild for state completeness — every state reachable by keyboard and pointer, focus visible in bright and dark contexts — while Ingvild's motion specifications are reviewed by Solveig for token compliance and regression capture. Rannveig's compositions are walked by Hrafnhildr; Hrafnhildr's flows are housed by Rannveig. No deliverable crosses the gate on a single family's judgment.

A worked pass makes the protocol concrete. Consider a hypothetical screen both families know well in kind: a Samtal permissions console — a Book XIII recipe surface — in which a hall keeper reviews agent trust scopes, grants or revokes capabilities, and sees each agent's identity card, current state, and human-readable capability summary under Book VIII. Family I leads the build. Rannveig composes it as Ship-Built Composition: one dominant focal anchor (the selected agent's trust panel), the agent list as left rail within the 220–288 px expanded band, detail in a 300–420 px context rail, one dominant action per task region. Solveig binds every value to Appendix B tokens, caps frame strengths at three, and registers the screen as a recipe instance with visual regression captures. Hallveig records the job in Tome 04's form: the keeper is hired to answer, in the user's language, "what can this agent do in my hall, and how do I change it?"

Family II reviews. Hrafnhildr walks the three most frequent journeys and finds the first defect: reaching the revoke control requires passing through the agent's activity timeline — backtracking through a working area, in Tome 06's terms — because the timeline was placed as the composition's visual centrepiece. The knife comes out: the timeline is demoted to the context rail, the trust panel takes the centre, and the journey shortens by two decisions. Ingvild inspects state visibility and finds the second defect: a granted capability and a pending capability differ only in accent hue — aurora versus forge gold — which is color-only meaning, forbidden by Book VII, Book XI, and Tome 09 alike. The repair is redundant encoding: label and shape join color, and the pending state gains a named structural motion within the 260–360 ms band to signal transition, with a complete reduced-motion alternate. Tove runs the gate last and finds the third defect: at 200% text scaling, the capability summary truncates, hiding the very disclosure Book VIII requires to be available; and the revoke control, set at compact 32 px height for density, fails the 44×44 px touch minimum on touch devices. She vetoes. The screen does not ship. Family I repairs — the summary reflows rather than truncates, the destructive control is separated from high-frequency actions, perceptually distinct by position, label, and form, not color alone, and set at standard height — and the screen returns through the full battery: grayscale, No-Rune, No-Glow, No-Art, Three-Second Silhouette, then long, empty, and failure states, including the state in which an agent's runtime is unreachable and its card must show failure and recovery as a designed surface. Only then does Hallveig bless it, and her blessing now means something precise: the system held, the task survived, and both were proven under removal, not merely admired under decoration. That sequence — compose, walk, inspect, veto, repair, re-prove — is the joint practice on an ordinary Tuesday. Its ordinariness is the achievement.

## Joint Rules

The following rules bind Families I and II jointly. SHALL denotes a ship condition; SHALL NOT denotes a defect. Each rule is testable by a reviewer who did not make the work.

1. Every screen SHALL name, in writing and before form work begins, the job it is hired for in the user's language, its one dominant focal anchor, and its one visually dominant action per task region; a screen whose job cannot be named in one sentence SHALL be restructured before visual work proceeds.
2. All values in product components SHALL resolve to named semantic tokens under Book XII governance; magic values SHALL fail review, and token changes SHALL ship with version and migration, never as silent local edits.
3. Every hall SHALL be declared as Longhouse Composition or Ship-Built Composition, SHALL hold one dominant focal centre, and SHALL NOT present two or more elements at equal maximum visual intensity competing as centres.
4. Circulation SHALL be audited before styling: the three most frequent journeys SHALL be traced with their decision points recorded, and any journey requiring backtracking through a working area SHALL be redesigned.
5. Working content — message streams, editors, tables, reading text — SHALL sit in the calm centre; ornament, background art, and texture SHALL NOT be placed behind working content at any density, and message width SHALL be optimized for reading.
6. When theme competes with task, or scope must shrink, reduction SHALL follow the fixed order — ornament, glow, texture, motion, and symbolic density first; ceremony and features next; the job, the information architecture, and accessibility last and only with the loss recorded — and navigation SHALL NOT be simplified to make room for theme expression.
7. Every themed element SHALL perform at least one measurable job — orientation, state, affordance, or change — and any element performing none SHALL be removed.
8. Colour SHALL NOT be the only indicator of status, presence, error, or selection; every colour signal SHALL be paired with a label, shape, icon, position, or text equivalent, perceivable by screen reader and in grayscale.
9. Touch targets SHALL be at least 44×44 px and standard controls 40–48 px high; body text SHALL be 14–18 px, with 11–13 px microtext carrying nonessential content only; contrast SHALL meet WCAG AA or better in every theme state.
10. Motion SHALL be causal, SHALL name what changed, and SHALL conform to the bands — micro 80–140 ms, standard 160–220 ms, structural 260–360 ms, ceremonial 450–800 ms and rare — and every animated surface SHALL provide a complete reduced-motion alternate state that preserves all meaning.
11. Empty, loading, error, offline, degraded, and failure-recovery states SHALL be designed, written in plain language, and tested as first-class surfaces; a design proven only on ideal content SHALL be recorded as unproven.
12. For every consequential action, the design SHALL name the foreseeable slip, lapse, and mistake, and SHALL provide a constraint against the slip, visible state against the lapse, and a plain-language recovery path; destructive controls SHALL be distinct by position, label, and form, not by colour alone, and SHALL be separated from high-frequency actions.
13. Any addition of motion, sound, badges, notifications, or ambient theatre SHALL name the attention it costs and the element removed or quieted to pay for it; workload SHALL be treated as a budget.
14. Agents SHALL be designed as citizens with visible identity, state, capability, and trust scope in human-readable form; no agent surface SHALL invite trust beyond its disclosed capability.
15. No deliverable SHALL be graded by its makers alone; every I-led deliverable SHALL pass II review and every II-led deliverable SHALL pass I review, through the acceptance battery, the Book XIV removal tests, and the long, empty, and failure state tests, closing with the Master Screen Review.
16. No surface SHALL pass the Book XIV gate while a known defect of target, contrast, scaling, motion, colour-only meaning, or memory demand remains unresolved; Tove's veto on such a defect SHALL stand until repair and re-test, and Hallveig's refusal to bless an unproven or unrecorded pattern SHALL stand until the pattern is proven and entered as a recipe, token, or contract.
